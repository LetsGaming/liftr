# Lens 1: Liftoff vs. Liftr — Design Philosophy Comparison

This is an independent research pass. It was produced cold, without reading any prior audit conclusions in `audit/`. All claims below are either (a) directly observed with a cited source, (b) sourced to a named external reference with a link, or (c) explicitly labelled as judgement, in which case the design principle it rests on is named. Where evidence is insufficient, this is stated explicitly rather than guessed at.

Liftoff is treated here as one competitor product under study, not as a standard to imitate. Liftr is treated as another artifact under study, not as "our app" — neither is assumed correct because it currently exists.

## 2. Liftoff's design philosophy, stated as explicit rules

### 2A. Visual language & hierarchy

- **Rule: dark, cool-toned base surface everywhere.** Every screen across the 89 flow frames and 17 screenshots uses a near-black background with a cool violet/navy undertone, never pure black and never a light theme (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-174544.png`, `examples/walkthrough_bundle/frames/frame_001.jpg`).
- **Rule: cards are distinguished by a lightness step, not borders or shadows**, except where a border is deliberately used as a "this is special" signal (see below) (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-174544.png`).
- **Rule: color is used as a functional code, not decoration.** Sky-blue = primary CTA / XP-level ring; orange/amber = streak flame and consistency-goal elements; purple = XP/duration stat fill; red/crimson = volume stat and (in one screenshot) the day's hero card; metallic gold/silver/bronze gradients are reserved specifically for rank-tier badges (`examples/ReactNative-snapshot-image4658256710183484041.png`, `examples/walkthrough_bundle/frames/frame_048.jpg`, `frame_057.jpg`).
- **Rule: a rainbow gradient outline marks a card as a gamified progress hook.** The placement-prompt card ("Rangiere 2 weitere Übungen...") and a level-up teaser card both use a violet→cyan→green→yellow→orange gradient ring not used anywhere else in the UI (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-175339.png`, `examples/walkthrough_bundle/frames/frame_034.jpg`). Judgement: this functions as a "rare/special" visual grammar borrowed from loot/gacha game UI conventions, distinguishing progress-and-reward surfaces from ordinary content surfaces — this is an inference from the visual pattern, not a stated design intent.
- **Rule: two illustration systems coexist and are used for different purposes.** Flat, saturated cartoon "sticker" icons represent exercises/food/objects; a second, more painterly anatomical body-diagram renderer (grey/white body, color-filled by muscle group) is the app's recurring signature motif, appearing in the dashboard recovery module, exercise detail, rank body-graph, workout summary, and profile (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-174559.png`, `examples/walkthrough_bundle/frames/frame_025.jpg`–`frame_037.jpg`). A third, higher-fidelity semi-realistic muscle render is used narrowly inside the exercise "how to perform" card only.
- **Rule: numeric emphasis follows a fixed pattern** — a large bold number stacked over a small muted unit/label (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-175324.png`, `frame_041.jpg`).
- **Rule: the header is persistent, dense, and always gamification-forward.** Avatar+level ring, streak flame counter, currency counter, and a bell/help icon are present on every single screen in the 89-frame flow, regardless of what task is being performed (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-174544.png` onward).
- **Rule: bottom navigation gives ranks and social features top-level, permanent real estate.** The 6-item tab bar is Workout, Home, Ränge (Ranks), Ernährung (Nutrition), Freunde (Friends), Profil — i.e. Ranks and Friends sit at the same navigational tier as core workout logging (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-174544.png`).

### 2B. Animation & transition vocabulary

Evidence source: all 73 filmstrips in `examples/walkthrough_bundle/animations/`, each spanning a 0.6s window of the source video; frame spacing within a strip indicates relative velocity. Roughly a third of the 73 filmstrips (e.g. `filmstrip_11_t082,4s.jpg`–`filmstrip_16_t098,4s.jpg`, `filmstrip_41_t160,3s.jpg`–`filmstrip_46_t172,5s.jpg`, `filmstrip_49_t195,6s.jpg`–`filmstrip_58_t222,5s.jpg`, `filmstrip_60_t230,4s.jpg`–`filmstrip_63_t242,9s.jpg`, `filmstrip_65_t247,5s.jpg`–`filmstrip_70_t288,9s.jpg`) show frames that are pixel-near-identical across the full window — these are excluded from the claims below since no motion character can be read from them; this concentration means the *density* of observable motion in the sampled walkthrough is lower than "73 filmstrips" suggests.

- **Rule: state-confirmation toasts snap in fast and hold.** The "Änderungen erfolgreich gespeichert!" toast is already fully visible by the second sampled frame in `filmstrip_06_t051,1s.jpg`–`filmstrip_08_t056,2s.jpg`, then holds static — consistent with a sub-200ms entrance and a multi-second hold, not a slow ease-in.
- **Rule: screen and mode transitions are fast cuts, not lingering slides/fades.** Back-navigation (`filmstrip_09_t058,5s.jpg`) and entry into profile edit mode (`filmstrip_64_t245,2s.jpg`) both resolve within a frame or two of the 0.6s window, with no crossfade or slide residue visible mid-transition.
- **Rule: loading states use shimmer skeletons with a data-dependent (not animation-timed) duration.** Grey placeholder blocks with a diagonal sweep highlight persist for a variable number of frames before a fast (~100–200ms) crossfade to real content, observed for analytics charts, a muscle heatmap, and a rank-history chart (`filmstrip_37_t153,0s.jpg`–`filmstrip_40_t158,8s.jpg`, `filmstrip_59_t225,9s.jpg`, `filmstrip_71_t292,8s.jpg`–`filmstrip_72_t293,7s.jpg`).
- **Rule: charts draw in faster than they can be sampled.** Both the hexagonal radar chart (`filmstrip_59_t225,9s.jpg`) and the rank-over-time line chart (`filmstrip_73_t303,1s.jpg`) show an already-complete shape by the second frame — the draw-in itself is sub-200ms, so Liftoff favors quick reveals over animated data storytelling.
- **Rule: list building uses per-item staggered insertion, not batch updates.** A cluster of 7 separate filmstrips within roughly one second of source video (`filmstrip_17_t100,5s.jpg` through `filmstrip_23_t101,5s.jpg`) each capture one discrete exercise being added to a routine — i.e. each addition needed its own ~150ms-scale filmstrip, evidencing a staggered per-item animation rather than one list re-render.
- **Rule: the richest compound animation moment is the level-up, and even there no confetti/particle/screen-flash effect was observed anywhere in the corpus.** `filmstrip_33_t122,3s.jpg` simultaneously shows a level-badge change, an XP-bar fill, a currency count-up, onboarding-checklist checkmarks, and a glowing gift-box icon — but the glow reads as a continuous ambient pulse (an "unclaimed reward" affordance loop) rather than a one-shot celebration burst. Across all 73 filmstrips, no confetti, particle burst, screen shake, or overshoot/bounce effect was found.
- **Rule: user-driven scrolling is linear/untransformed**, consistent with finger-tracked drag rather than a programmatic eased auto-scroll (`filmstrip_04_t037,9s.jpg`, `filmstrip_05_t038,5s.jpg`).

Overall, judged from this evidence: Liftoff's motion vocabulary is **utilitarian and fast at the micro-interaction level** (toasts, transitions, chart reveals all resolve in well under the 0.6s sampling window), with animated *choreography* reserved narrowly for list-building and the ambient reward-glow loop, rather than for one-shot celebratory bursts. This is a direct observation from the filmstrips, not an assumption about what a gamified app "should" do — the corpus specifically does not show confetti or particle effects, which is worth stating plainly since that is a common assumption about this genre of app.

### 2C. Engagement/retention mechanics ("baits")

All items below are directly observed on screen unless marked otherwise; web-research-only claims are marked with their source.

1. **XP + Level, always visible.** A level ring around the avatar sits in the persistent header on every screen (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-174544.png`). Workout summary shows "+434 XP" with an expandable "XP-Aufschlüsselung anzeigen" breakdown (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-175603.png`).
2. **Full-screen "LEVEL UP!" modal** with a mascot (a cartoon elephant), a currency reward ("6 Eier verdient!"), and an XP bar, gating on a "FERTIG" button (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-175603.png`).
3. **Streak flame, always visible in the header**, plus a distinct "Konsistenzziel" (consistency goal) dashboard widget with a 7-day pill row and a numeric weekly-streak target (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-175324.png`, `frame_042.jpg`). A separate, dedicated full-screen streak-celebration interstitial exists — pure black background, a giant numeral + flame, weekday pill row, share button, "FORTFAHREN" CTA (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-175558.png`). A streak-restore ("Wiederherstellen") pill was observed pinned above the header in a lapsed-streak state, implying an action- or paywall-gated recovery path (`examples/walkthrough_bundle/frames/frame_001.jpg`).
4. **A currency ("Eier"/eggs) counter, always visible in the header**, earned at level-ups and via an onboarding-quest "Gut Gemacht!" reveal modal with a radial reward-icon burst and a wrapped-gift graphic (`examples/walkthrough_bundle/frames/frame_037.jpg`–`frame_038.jpg`). A full cosmetics/inventory economy exists (Items/Titel/Themes/Grenzen/Banner tabs) with an in-app shop entry point (`frame_039.jpg`).
5. **A nine-tier ranked ladder (Holz→Bronze→Silber→Gold→Platin→Diamant→Champion→Titan→Olympia) applied independently to every tracked exercise**, each with its own LP score, visualized as a scrollable grid of metallic-gradient cards (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-175351.png`, `frame_048.jpg`–`frame_056.jpg`). A separate account-level aggregate rank exists per training category (weightlifting, calisthenics), each with its own rank-over-time line chart (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-175603.png`, `frame_088.jpg`–`frame_089.jpg`).
6. **A "placement" soft-gate**: "Rangiere 2 weitere Übungen, um deinen Liftoff Rang zu erhalten!" with a 10-hexagon progress tracker and the rainbow-gradient "special" outline, explicitly pushing continued engagement to unlock the headline account rank (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-175339.png`).
7. **A live, in-workout rank-progress teaser** embedded below the rest timer during active set logging, showing "ZUM NÄCHSTEN RANG: ???" with the exact numeric target masked — reward-adjacent stimulus delivered mid-set, not only at session end (`examples/mid-workout/Screenshot_20260824-175421.png` and 4 further mid-workout screenshots). The `???` masking recurs on the rank-detail screen too (`frame_045.jpg`, `frame_074.jpg`, `frame_081.jpg`), consistently paired with a progress bar that reads as nearly full — a deliberate near-miss/anticipation device: the user can see they're close without seeing exactly how close.
8. **An onboarding quest system**: a 4-item "Einstiegs-Aufgaben" checklist (complete tutorial, enable notifications, set profile picture, complete first workout) gated behind a "press to claim your gift" reveal, plus a parallel "First Week Challenge" with its own progress ring (`frame_035.jpg`, `frame_002.jpg`). Enabling notifications is explicitly one of the four gated tasks, i.e. the permission prompt itself is incentivized rather than presented neutrally.
9. **A paywall embedded inline inside the exercise/rank-prediction flow** (not a separate, skippable screen): a gradient CTA reading "...ranks entsperren" ("Unlock [...] ranks") sits directly beneath a "KOSTENLOS TESTEN" chip inside the rank-detail card the user is already engaging with (`frame_074.jpg`).
10. **Social/multi-user features are structural, not optional add-ons**: a dedicated "Freunde" bottom-nav tab; a Home segmented control for "Für dich / Freunde / Entdeckung"; dashboard tiles for "Bestenlisten" (leaderboards) and "Soziale Feeds — Mit der Welt trainieren"; global/regional percentile placement stats on every exercise's rank card; and a public profile format with a `@handle`, editable bio, and cosmetic titles/banners/borders (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-174544.png`, `frame_042.jpg`, `frame_053.jpg`, `frame_059.jpg`–`frame_060.jpg`).

**Web research supplement** (source: App Store listing, MWM publisher page, and one third-party design teardown — see full citations in §6):

- Confirmed name: "Liftoff – Ranked Gym Workouts," developer GymBros Inc., 1M+ users, 4.7–4.8★ (App Store listing: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/liftoff-ranked-gym-workouts/id6448081563).

### 2D. Reward staging & pacing

Reconstructed from the mid-workout screenshots, the streak/level-up screenshots (`examples/Screenshot_20260824-175558.png`, `.../175603.png`, both timestamped 17:55–17:56, immediately after the mid-workout screenshots at 17:54–17:55), and the onboarding-checklist frame sequence:

1. **During the set**: the masked "???" next-rank progress teaser is visible under the rest timer at all times; a "Ruhezeit ist vorbei!" modal interrupts with an OK button when the timer elapses — i.e. reward-adjacent stimuli occur mid-workout, not only at session end.
2. **End-of-session recap 1 — a full-screen, no-chrome streak interstitial** (huge numeral + flame + weekday strip + praise copy + share button + "FORTFAHREN"), appearing to fire immediately on completion, before any XP/level content is shown.
3. **End-of-session recap 2 — a second, separate full-screen interstitial** chaining directly after recap 1: streak-flame pips with a "+1 Eierbonus" callout → currency-earned banner ("6 Eier verdient!") → "LEVEL UP!" wordmark with mascot → XP bar with expandable breakdown → "FERTIG" to exit. Two distinct interstitials are chained back-to-back rather than merged into one screen.
4. **The general reward pattern observed for quest completion** (onboarding checklist reaching 100%, `frame_035.jpg` → `frame_036.jpg`) is: checklist/quest completion → an explicit tap-to-reveal gate ("Drücken um dein Geschenk zu erhalten!") → a currency/title reward-reveal modal with radial icon-burst animation. Rewards are consistently staged as dedicated full-screen or modal interstitials, never as an inline toast.
5. A separate, exportable **workout-summary share-card format** exists (`examples/ReactNative-snapshot-image4658256710183484041.png`), functioning as a distinct "ready to post" artifact downstream of the interstitials above.

## 3. Liftr's implicit design philosophy

All items below are directly observed live at `http://localhost:5174` on 2026-09-03 (dev build), plus the two share-card images. Liftr has no stated public design manifesto reviewed here; what follows is reconstructed from behavior, not from documentation.

### 3A. Visual language & hierarchy

- **Rule: dark navy base, functionally identical family to Liftoff's dark palette**, but with fewer distinct accent colors overall (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-overview-recovery-zone.jpg`). Judgement: a dark, cool-toned base is common across the fitness-tracker category generally and is not distinctive to either product on its own — no claim of similarity beyond "both are dark-themed" is warranted from this alone.
- **Rule: cards are flat-fill rounded rectangles, generally without the gradient/metallic treatment Liftoff reserves for rank cards** — except that Liftr's own per-exercise rank cards *do* use an orange-to-brown gradient fill closely resembling Liftoff's rank-tier card treatment (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-ranks-per-exercise-lp.jpg`, compare `examples/walkthrough_bundle/frames/frame_048.jpg`). This is flagged in §5 as a possible adopted-mechanic case.
- **Rule: the header carries exactly two persistent gamification elements — a level pill with a progress bar, and a streak-flame pill** (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-overview-stats-level-streak-rank.jpg`). This is narrower than Liftoff's four-icon header (level ring, streak, currency, notification bell) — Liftr shows no currency counter and no notification-bell icon in the persistent header.
- **Rule: the body-diagram muscle illustration is reused across Overview (recovery state), the active workout screen (muscles being trained), and the share card** — the same illustration technique (grey outline body, color-filled by muscle group) recurs in all three, similar in spirit to Liftoff's signature body-diagram motif but serving a different specific purpose in each place (recovery status vs. trained-muscle display vs. static share-card decoration) (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-overview-recovery-zone.jpg`, `liftr-workout-set-logging-lp-bar.jpg`, `examples/liftr/liftr-workout-f25435c3.png`).
- **Rule: bottom navigation has 5 tabs — Übersicht, Workout, Ränge, Übungen, Profil** — with "Läufe" (Runs) nested as a sub-tab inside Workout rather than being a top-level tab of its own (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-overview-stats-level-streak-rank.jpg` header + tab bar visible in all lens-1 screenshots). Notably, Liftr has no Friends/social tab and no Nutrition tab — the nav surface is narrower than Liftoff's 6-tab bar and contains nothing multi-user.
- **Rule: navigation reflows between a sidebar (desktop-width viewport) and a bottom tab bar (mobile-width viewport)** — observed directly: the same app at a ~1450px window rendered a left sidebar with "Übersicht / Workout / Ränge / Übungen / Profil," while at 390×844 it rendered the same five items as a bottom tab bar. This is a responsive-layout fact, not a judgement.

### 3B. Animation & transition vocabulary

- **Not extensively determinable from this pass.** The live walkthrough captured static end-states via screenshots, not motion; no equivalent of Liftoff's 73-filmstrip corpus was collected for Liftr, and this was outside the scope given to this research task (the task explicitly directs filmstrip analysis at Liftoff only). What can be said from direct observation: rest-timer countdown updates numerically each second (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-workout-set-saved-rest-timer.jpg` shows "1:30" and "1:28" a few seconds apart in two separate captures), and set-save produces an inline "+XX XP" numeral appearing next to the save button rather than a full-screen or modal interstitial (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-workout-set-logging-lp-bar.jpg` vs. a later capture showing "+230 XP" adjacent to "Satz speichern"). Whether this XP numeral animates (fade, count-up, pop) or simply appears is **not determinable from static screenshots**.

### 3C. Engagement/retention mechanics

1. **A level system (Lv. N, with an X/Y-to-next-level fraction) is present and always visible in the header**, functionally analogous to Liftoff's level ring (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-overview-stats-level-streak-rank.jpg`).
2. **XP is awarded per set, shown as an inline "+N XP" numeral next to the set-save button**, not as a modal (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-workout-set-logging-lp-bar.jpg`, and the earlier in-session capture showing "+230 XP" / "+86 XP" / "+56 XP" / "+29 XP" for different sets — note the amounts vary per set, suggesting XP is computed from load/reps/effort rather than being a flat per-set reward).
3. **A streak counter ("X Tage Serie") is present in the header**, incrementing after a completed workout (observed going from "1 Tage" to "2 Tage" across two separate workout completions in this session) — no dedicated full-screen streak-celebration interstitial was observed; the streak simply updates in the persistent header pill (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-workout-finish-progress-xp.jpg` header vs. `examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-overview-stats-level-streak-rank.jpg` header).
4. **A per-exercise rank system exists** ("LEHRLING III," "95 LP," visible during active set-logging directly above the weight/reps input) with an explicit "Nächstes Ziel: 36.25 kg × 9" numeric target shown plainly, unmasked (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-workout-set-logging-lp-bar.jpg`). This contrasts directly with Liftoff's masked "???" next-rank target (§2C item 7) — Liftr shows the exact number needed to rank up, with no near-miss/anticipation device observed anywhere in this pass.
5. **A full nine-tier rank ladder exists** (Anfänger → Lehrling → Auszubildender → Sportler → Heber → Fortgeschritten → Elite → Experte → Apex), each with a stated "N Stufen" (N sub-stages) shown on the Ranks tab, plus an explicit accompanying line of stated intent: **"Pro Übung · echte Standards wo verfügbar, sonst abgeleitet — nichts gesperrt"** ("Per exercise: real standards where available, otherwise derived — nothing locked") (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-ranks-tier-ladder.jpg`). This is a directly-observed, on-screen textual design claim, not an inference — it explicitly states a "nothing is gated behind rank" policy, in direct contrast to Liftoff's placement soft-gate (§2C item 6) and paywalled rank features (§2C item 9).
6. **The Profile screen contains an explicit, on-screen disclaimer about the XP/level system's stakes**: "Zusätzlich zum Rangsystem — nichts hängt davon ab, kann jederzeit ausgeblendet werden" ("In addition to the rank system — nothing depends on it, can be hidden at any time") (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-profile-xp-level-section.jpg`). This is a directly observed, explicit design statement inside the product itself, distinguishing XP/levels as optional flavor rather than a core mechanic — no equivalent disclaimer was observed anywhere in the Liftoff material.
7. **No currency, no cosmetics/inventory system, no shop, no gift-box reveal mechanic, no mascot, and no notification-permission gamification were observed anywhere in the Liftr walkthrough.**
8. **An explicit anti-farming/anti-gaming statement was observed on the workout-finish screen after an unusually fast test session**: "Diese Session wirkte ungewöhnlich schnell — Rang- und XP-Gewinn wurden reduziert" ("This session seemed unusually fast — rank and XP gain were reduced") (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-workout-finish-stats-anti-farming-note.jpg`). This is a directly observed, on-screen mechanic actively working against reward-maximizing behavior — no equivalent was found anywhere in the Liftoff material, where no rate-limiting or anti-farming messaging was observed in 89 frames + 17 screenshots + 5 mid-workout captures.
9. **A "you were stronger than planned" adaptive-routine prompt** appears on the finish screen when actual performance exceeds the routine's programmed target, offering "Routine aktualisieren" (update the routine) or "Nicht jetzt" (not now) (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-workout-finish-stats-anti-farming-note.jpg`). This is a data-driven progression suggestion tied to actual lifted numbers, not a rank/XP mechanic per se.
10. **No leaderboards, no friends list, no public profile, no social feed, and no percentile/"Top X%" comparison against other users were observed anywhere in the Liftr walkthrough.** The Profile screen's copy is explicit about this posture: "Dein Server, dein Konto, deine Daten" ("Your server, your account, your data") (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-profile-settings-bodyweight-training.jpg`), and the Runs tab's empty-state copy states "Als Datei importiert · deine Daten, kein Drittanbieter-Konto" ("Imported as a file — your data, no third-party account") (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-runs-empty-state.jpg`).

### 3D. Reward staging & pacing

Directly observed across two independently-run test workouts (identical routine, both completed in this session):

1. **During the set**: an inline "+N XP" numeral appears next to the "Satz speichern" (Save Set) button immediately on save; a rest timer then appears inline in the same scrolling page (not a modal), counting down with a skippable "Überspringen" button (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-workout-set-logging-lp-bar.jpg`, `liftr-workout-set-saved-rest-timer.jpg`).
2. **On tapping "Workout beenden" (End Workout)**: a full-screen (but still within normal app chrome — header/tab-bar remain visible) reward card appears first. **This varied between the two test runs**: one run showed a "RANGAUFSTIEGE" (Rank-ups) card naming the specific exercise that ranked up plus "Neuer Rekord" (New Record) with a "Weiter tippen →" (tap to continue) prompt (observed live, not saved to disk); the second run instead showed a "FORTSCHRITT" (Progress) card showing "+278 XP" and the current level bar with the same "Weiter tippen →" prompt (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-workout-finish-progress-xp.jpg`). This indicates the finish sequence is conditionally staged — a rank-up is shown when one occurred, otherwise a generic XP/progress card is shown — rather than always presenting the same fixed sequence of screens Liftoff uses (§2D item 3, which chains two interstitials unconditionally).
3. **Tapping through** leads to a "GESCHAFFT" (Done) summary screen: routine name, a per-exercise rank-progress row (still within normal chrome, not full-screen-takeover, not black-background), the trained-muscle body diagram, an adaptive-routine prompt if applicable, and three flat numeric stat tiles (duration, volume, set count) (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-workout-finish-geschafft-summary.jpg`, `liftr-workout-finish-stats-anti-farming-note.jpg`).
4. **Exit options at the bottom of the finish screen**: "Als Bild teilen" (share as image), "In Zwischenablage kopieren" (copy to clipboard), and "Fertig" (Done) — sharing is offered but not gated or emphasized as a primary action; "Fertig" sits directly below the share options with equal visual weight (from the earlier live walkthrough, not re-saved to disk but directly observed).

Judgement, naming the principle: Liftoff's reward sequence is unconditionally staged as two dedicated, full-screen (black-background, chrome-hidden) interstitials every time, functioning as a mandatory "moment" the user must pass through (per the interruption-cost principle: forcing an uninterruptible full-screen state maximizes the reward's felt weight but also maximizes the cost imposed on users who just want to close the app). Liftr's reward sequence stays within normal app chrome and appears to vary its content by what actually happened (rank-up vs. plain progress) rather than presenting a fixed ritual — this reduces the felt ceremony of any single reward but also reduces the interruption cost per session. Neither approach is inherently correct; they trade off differently depending on whether repeat-open frequency or per-session ceremony is the thing being optimized for, and this pass makes no claim about which optimization target is more appropriate for either product.

### 3E. Empty / first-run states

- **Runs (Läufe) empty state**: "Noch keine Läufe erfasst. Importiere eine GPX- oder FIT-Datei aus deiner Uhr oder App, oder trage einen Lauf manuell nach," with a single "GPX/FIT importieren" CTA and a "Manuell" toggle already visible (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-runs-empty-state.jpg`). No onboarding checklist, no reward-gated first action, no mascot.
- **Körpergewicht (bodyweight) empty state on Profile**: "Noch kein Eintrag — Rang-Berechnung nutzt vorläufig 75 kg" ("No entry yet — rank calculation provisionally uses 75 kg") — a plain-language statement of the fallback the system is using, rather than a prompt to complete a task for a reward (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-profile-settings-bodyweight-training.jpg`).
- **Routine-creation wizard first screen** offers exactly two paths with descriptive body copy each — "Selbst zusammenstellen" (build it yourself) and "Nach Muskelgruppe vorschlagen lassen" (let Liftr suggest by muscle group) — no quest framing, no reward preview (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-routine-wizard-step1-path-chooser.jpg`).
- No dedicated "first steps" checklist, no tap-to-reveal gift mechanic, and no feature-education popup were observed anywhere in this pass — **absence noted as a direct observation of what was and wasn't encountered during the walkthrough**, not proof that no such thing exists anywhere in the app (a fresh, zero-workout account state was not specifically tested, since the account already had 4 levels and 2 workouts on record going in).

### 3F. Share-card comparison (old vs. current), from the two files reviewed

- **Old** (`examples/liftr/old/liftr-workout-b628871a.png`): plain numeric stats with no card framing (just colored numerals over labels), no rank badge shown at all, exercise list truncated to 3 rows with a "+1 weitere Übungen" overflow line, muscle diagram present but no rank/level context anywhen on the card.
- **Current** (`examples/liftr/liftr-workout-f25435c3.png`): the same four stats are now individually outlined stat cards (purple/blue/orange/yellow borders, each with a label pill and a dark inset number box), a gold hexagonal rank badge with the rank name and level number was added beneath the stat row, and the exercise list now shows all logged exercises with full per-set breakdowns rather than truncating.
- Judgement, naming the principle (visual hierarchy / progressive disclosure): the current card adds more gamification surface (a rank badge, individually bordered stat cards resembling Liftoff's colored stat-card convention) than the old one had, while also becoming more complete/less truncated on the factual data (full exercise list). This is a factual comparison of two specific files, not a claim about the app's overall trajectory beyond what these two images show.

## 5. Flags

### 5A. Liftr mechanics that visually echo a Liftoff-style mechanic

- **Per-exercise rank cards using an orange/brown gradient-fill treatment** (`examples/liftr/lens-1/liftr-ranks-per-exercise-lp.jpg`) closely resembles Liftoff's metallic-gradient rank-tier card convention (`examples/walkthrough_bundle/frames/frame_048.jpg`). Judgement: on its own this is a plausibly-independent design choice (gradient "medal" styling is a common way to visually signal a ranked/tiered achievement across many products, not unique to Liftoff), and the surrounding context differs substantially — Liftr's version carries an explicit numeric target and an unlocked-by-default framing ("nichts gesperrt"), which is the opposite of the placement-gate and paywall context that gives Liftoff's equivalent cards their retention function. If this visual similarity was an intentional reference, it has been decoupled from the mechanic (gating, near-miss masking, placement quotas) that gives it its engagement-engineering purpose in Liftoff — the surface look was potentially kept, the underlying retention machinery was not.
- **The "Neuer Rekord" (New Record) rank-up card and "Weiter tippen →" (tap to continue) prompt** on Liftr's workout-finish screen (observed live) functions similarly to Liftoff's tap-gated reward reveals (`frame_036.jpg`'s "Gut Gemacht!" reveal, gated behind a tap). This is a much narrower and shallower echo than a full onboarding-quest system — Liftr's version is a single card with a single continue-tap, not a multi-step gated reveal sequence, and it appears conditionally (only shown when a rank-up actually occurred) rather than as a designed-in ritual.

### 5B. Liftoff mechanics that structurally depend on multi-user/social scale

These cannot be meaningfully built for a single-user, no-accounts, self-hosted product without changing its fundamental architecture:

- **Leaderboards ("Bestenlisten")** — both the global dashboard tile and the per-exercise "Global --/Top --%" percentile stat require a population of other users' data to compute at all (`frame_042.jpg`, `frame_053.jpg`).
- **"Soziale Feeds" (Social Feeds)** — explicitly framed as "Mit der Welt trainieren" (train with the world); requires other users' activity to populate (`frame_042.jpg`).
- **Friends tab and Friends/Discovery segmented navigation** — requires a friend graph (`Screenshot_20260824-174544.png`, `Screenshot_20260824-175320.png`).
- **Public profile pages with `@handle` format, viewable by others** — the entire screen's value proposition assumes an audience (`frame_059.jpg`–`frame_060.jpg`).
- **Regional/global percentile rank display** — mathematically requires a population to rank against (`frame_053.jpg`).
- **Share-template and streak-share icons** — technically function without other users (export to an external share sheet), but their value proposition is inherently about being seen by others, which a self-hosted single-user tool has no built-in audience for (`frame_007.jpg`, `Screenshot_20260824-175558.png`). Liftr's own "Als Bild teilen" share option (§3D item 4) is architecturally similar in mechanism (export an image) but not gated or emphasized as a primary flow, and does not imply an in-app audience the way Liftoff's social-feed-oriented share icons do.

None of these were observed anywhere in the Liftr walkthrough, and Liftr's server architecture (single bearer token, no accounts, no sessions, per `packages/server/src/auth.ts`) makes them structurally unbuildable without a fundamentally different backend — this is a direct architectural fact, not a design preference, and is the clearest hard boundary in this whole comparison.

## 6. Open questions / evidence gaps

- Liftr's actual login/AuthGate screen was never observed (dev environment has no token configured) — **not determinable from available evidence** whether it exists, and if so what it looks like.
- Liftr's live animation/transition behavior (easing, duration, whether the "+XX XP" numeral animates) could not be assessed from static screenshots — no equivalent filmstrip evidence was collected for Liftr in this pass, since the task scope directed filmstrip analysis at Liftoff specifically.
- Liftoff's actual backend anti-abuse behavior (rate-limiting, fraud detection) is unknown — the absence of user-facing messaging about it in the reviewed material does not establish its absence server-side.
- The identification of the screenshotted app as "Liftoff – Ranked Gym Workouts" / GymBros Inc. is an inference from visual/branding match, not from an explicit source URL in the task material; treated as correct throughout, but flagged as an inference.
- Liftoff's stated design philosophy in its own words (vs. third-party interpretation) was not found anywhere in the web research — the only source characterizing Liftoff's underlying design thesis ("tracks where you stand" vs. "what you do") is a single third-party teardown (tasu.ai), not a company statement, and is treated throughout as one analyst's opinion, not fact.
- Individual, verbatim user reviews of Liftoff could not be retrieved (App Store review page and a third-party aggregator both returned errors or only tool-summarized content) — all review-sentiment claims in §2C are secondhand summaries, not independently verified quotes.
- Whether Liftr's fresh-account / zero-workout empty states include any onboarding-checklist-style flow was not tested, since the test account already had prior level/workout history; §3E's "no onboarding checklist observed" is an observation of what wasn't encountered on an established account, not confirmation that none exists for a brand-new one.
- No claim is made in this document about which product's approach produces better outcomes (retention, habit formation, user wellbeing, or otherwise) — that would require behavioral/outcome data neither this pass nor the source material contains.
